# RWAS Avionics Customer Lifecycle Design

**Date:** August 9, 2026  
**Status:** Approved design  
**Scope:** Manufacturer-neutral avionics-upgrade customer journey, internal RWAS operating workflow, future webpage narrative, and future sales-film structure  
**Primary location:** RWAS at KYKN, Yankton, South Dakota

## 1. Purpose

This design defines one controlled RWAS customer lifecycle from initial avionics vision through return to service (RTS) and post-delivery support. It must work for customers who begin through the website, RWAS Panel Planner, telephone, referral, event, or walk-in inquiry.

The same lifecycle supports four audiences without creating four conflicting processes:

1. Aircraft owners need a clear, confidence-building journey.
2. RWAS personnel need assigned responsibilities, records, approvals, and completion gates.
3. The customer portal needs accurate, plain-language project statuses.
4. Future marketing needs a webpage and video narrative grounded in actual shop operations.

The workflow is manufacturer-neutral. Garmin equipment and systems are used as important examples because RWAS is an authorized Garmin dealer, but the master process applies to all supported avionics manufacturers.

## 2. Approved design decisions

- Use a synchronized **two-lane lifecycle**: customer experience and RWAS execution.
- Accept inquiries from every current channel, then route them into one standardized intake process.
- Assign one **RWAS Project Advisor** as the customer’s primary contact from intake through the 90-day follow-up.
- Provide milestone updates and at least one project update each week while the aircraft is in active work.
- Notify the customer immediately when a finding affects cost, schedule, configuration, scope, or airworthiness.
- Perform no additional billable work without documented customer approval.
- Show payment timing as generic milestones rather than fixed percentages.
- Include an in-flight customer familiarization and operational demonstration when aircraft condition, weather, pilot qualifications, and scope permit. Provide a ground-based alternative when flight is impractical.
- Use a manufacturer-neutral delivery checklist with equipment-specific supplements.
- Incorporate Garmin Customer Aircraft Delivery Checklist 190-03194-00 Revision 4 for applicable Garmin projects.
- Provide structured follow-up at 48–72 hours, 30 days, and 90 days after delivery.
- Use a cinematic customer-facing narrative that can become both a webpage and a 75–90 second sales film.

## 3. Design principles

### 3.1 Mission before equipment

The project begins with how the customer flies, what the current aircraft does poorly, what capabilities matter, and what success looks like. Equipment selection follows the mission rather than leading it.

### 3.2 One promise, one operating process

The website, sales presentation, customer portal, work order, approvals, delivery checklist, and post-RTS support must describe the same lifecycle. Marketing must not promise a status, timeline, or deliverable that the operating process cannot support.

### 3.3 Clear authority boundaries

The Project Advisor owns communication and continuity. Appropriately qualified personnel own technical work, inspection, and RTS decisions. A scheduled delivery date or completed installation does not itself constitute RTS.

### 3.4 No surprise work

Unexpected findings are normal in aircraft modification projects. Surprise billing is not. RWAS documents findings, consequences, options, price, and schedule impact before additional billable work proceeds.

### 3.5 Delivery is a capability transfer

The aircraft is not merely handed back. RWAS transfers the records, configuration knowledge, training resources, connected-account setup, operational understanding, and support path the customer needs to use the upgrade successfully.

### 3.6 Support continues after RTS

RTS ends the maintenance release process; it does not end the customer relationship. Early follow-up catches training, configuration, connectivity, database, warranty, and technical issues before frustration grows.

## 4. Roles

These are functional roles. One person may hold multiple roles when qualified and authorized.

### Customer

- Describes the mission, priorities, budget range, and timing.
- Supplies accurate aircraft identity, records, photographs, and known-condition information.
- Reviews concepts, alternatives, scope, and assumptions.
- Approves or declines proposals and scope changes.
- Coordinates aircraft delivery and pickup.
- Participates in account setup, delivery orientation, and follow-up.
- Reports abnormal behavior or questions promptly after delivery.

### RWAS Project Advisor

- Serves as the customer’s single primary contact.
- Owns intake, discovery, expectations, recaps, status communication, and decision routing.
- Coordinates specialists without representing unapproved technical conclusions as final.
- Maintains continuity through the 90-day follow-up.

### Lead Avionics Technician or Project Engineer

- Reviews feasibility, approved-data path, system compatibility, structure, electrical loading, interfaces, antennas, fabrication, installation risks, and checkout requirements.
- Defines and supervises the technical plan within assigned authority.
- Ensures technical findings reach the Project Advisor and work-order record.

### Service Writer or Work-Order Coordinator

- Converts the accepted project into a controlled work order.
- Maintains scope, schedule, labor/parts coordination, discrepancies, customer approvals, and closeout readiness.

### Parts Coordinator

- Confirms part numbers, availability, lead times, receiving, staging, traceability, and project kit readiness.
- Escalates backorders or substitutions before they affect the committed schedule.

### Installation Team

- Performs removal, fabrication, wiring, installation, configuration, testing, and work recording under applicable procedures and supervision.

### Inspector

- Performs required inspections and quality-control review within assigned authority.
- Records findings and prevents incomplete work from advancing to release.

### Authorized RTS Signer

- Reviews the applicable work and records.
- Approves return to service only when authorized and when all applicable requirements are satisfied.

### Delivery Technician or Qualified Pilot

- Conducts delivery-day system setup and operational demonstration.
- Participates in the familiarization flight only within applicable qualifications, operating rules, and agreed scope.

## 5. Master ten-phase lifecycle

### Phase 1: Connect and Qualify

**Customer experience**

- Begin through the website, Panel Planner, phone, referral, event, or walk-in.
- Receive an acknowledgment and a named Project Advisor.
- Understand the immediate next step and information RWAS needs.

**RWAS execution**

- Create or match the customer and aircraft record.
- Capture lead source and contact consent.
- Record basic aircraft and project information.
- Confirm that the requested work appears to fit RWAS capabilities and service area.
- Route urgent maintenance or safety concerns outside the ordinary sales path when necessary.

**Deliverable:** Qualified lead record.  
**Exit gate:** RWAS confirms the request is appropriate for discovery.

### Phase 2: Discover the Mission

**Customer experience**

- Explain current frustrations, normal missions, IFR/VFR needs, passenger and workload concerns, desired capabilities, timing, and budget range.
- Identify must-have, preferred, and future capabilities.

**RWAS execution**

- Lead a structured mission conversation.
- Separate operational goals from specific equipment requests.
- Identify certification category and likely approval constraints.
- Define project success in customer language.
- Issue a written discovery recap.

**Deliverable:** Customer mission brief and success criteria.  
**Exit gate:** Customer and RWAS agree on the problem the project should solve.

### Phase 3: Create the Vision

**Customer experience**

- Supply aircraft photographs, panel photos, equipment list, records, aircraft year, make, model, serial number, and N-number.
- Build a concept in the RWAS Panel Planner or work with RWAS through a guided concept session.
- Compare layout and capability alternatives without treating the sketch as approved engineering.

**RWAS execution**

- Preserve the original customer concept and later revisions.
- Inventory the existing panel and interfaced systems.
- Identify missing information that prevents review.
- Convert the customer’s preferences into a structured concept package.

**Deliverable:** Saved concept, aircraft baseline information, and revision history.  
**Exit gate:** Customer confirms that the concept represents the intended vision.

### Phase 4: Engineer the Solution

**Customer experience**

- Receive clear explanations of conflicts, dependencies, tradeoffs, and alternatives.
- Understand what is confirmed and what remains an assumption until aircraft induction.

**RWAS execution**

- Review STC/AML or other eligibility and approved-data path.
- Review panel structure, equipment dimensions and behind-panel clearance.
- Review electrical load, circuit protection, buses, backup power, and interfaces.
- Review antenna, sensor, servo, control, cooling, and fabrication requirements.
- Review retained equipment, removal scope, software, databases, and connected services.
- Identify schedule, parts, certification, and installation risks.
- Build the proposed configuration and explicit alternatives.

**Deliverable:** Technically reviewed solution, assumptions, exclusions, alternatives, and risk list.  
**Exit gate:** RWAS confirms the proposed solution is supportable enough to quote.

### Phase 5: Approve and Schedule

**Customer experience**

- Receive a proposal tied to the aircraft and mission rather than a cart of equipment.
- Review scope, equipment, labor assumptions, exclusions, customer responsibilities, schedule assumptions, generic payment milestones, and change-order rules.
- Approve, decline, or request revisions.

**RWAS execution**

- Prepare the customer proposal and project schedule.
- Confirm quote validity and equipment lead times.
- Record accepted configuration and revision.
- Record proposal acceptance and required initial payment milestone.
- Open or advance the controlled work order.

**Generic payment milestones**

1. Proposal acceptance and scheduling milestone.
2. Equipment, parts, or progress milestone(s).
3. Final delivery milestone.

Percentages and exact payment terms are business-policy values outside this design.

**Deliverable:** Accepted proposal and scheduled work order.  
**Exit gate:** Acceptance, required payment milestone, and schedule are recorded.

### Phase 6: Prepare and Induct

**Customer experience**

- Receive aircraft-arrival instructions and training/account-preparation resources.
- Coordinate aircraft delivery, keys, records, access, fuel expectations, and pickup constraints.
- Receive confirmation when the aircraft is accepted into the project.

**RWAS execution before arrival**

- Order, receive, inspect, trace, and stage the project equipment.
- Confirm kit completeness and backorder status.
- Prepare drawings, data, work instructions, and anticipated checkout requirements.
- Prepare the customer account and training checklist applicable to the equipment.

**RWAS execution at induction**

- Confirm aircraft identity and records.
- Document aircraft condition and current configuration.
- Reconcile the quoted assumptions with the actual aircraft.
- Record pre-existing conditions and induction findings.
- Confirm customer account permissions when connected services require them.
- Establish the authorized work baseline before removal or modification begins.

**Deliverable:** Staged project, induction record, and confirmed aircraft baseline.  
**Exit gate:** Aircraft condition and authorized scope are reconciled; unresolved findings are routed for decision.

### Phase 7: Build and Communicate

**Customer experience**

- Receive progress updates at major milestones and at least weekly.
- See useful photographs when appropriate.
- Receive immediate, understandable requests when a decision is required.
- Approve, decline, or request discussion of additional work.

**RWAS execution**

- Remove equipment according to scope.
- Fabricate the panel and supporting parts.
- Build or modify harnesses and wiring.
- Install equipment, antennas, sensors, controls, circuit protection, and placards.
- Configure systems and preserve configuration records.
- Record labor, parts, inspections, tests, photographs, and work entries.
- Maintain tool, parts, and aircraft protection controls.
- Keep the work order and customer-visible status aligned.

**Customer update format**

Every update answers:

1. What has been completed?
2. What happens next?
3. Is a customer decision required?
4. Has expected cost or schedule changed?

**Change-order record**

- Finding or requested change
- Why it matters
- Photographs or evidence when useful
- Recommended action and reasonable alternatives
- Cost impact
- Schedule impact
- Approve, decline, or request-discussion decision
- Approver and timestamp

**Deliverable:** Completed installation record with resolved customer decisions.  
**Exit gate:** Installation is complete and no scope decision blocks checkout.

### Phase 8: Verify and Return to Service

**Customer experience**

- Receive notice that the project has entered testing and inspection.
- Understand that installation completion is not the same as RTS.
- Receive prompt communication if testing reveals a new issue or schedule impact.

**RWAS execution**

- Complete required system checkout and post-installation procedures in applicable installation manuals and approved data.
- Confirm software, configuration, labels, interfaces, alerts, sensors, and operating modes.
- Close functional-test and inspection findings.
- Complete logbook entries, weight and balance, Instructions for Continued Airworthiness, wiring package, AFMS, FAA forms, release documents, and other applicable records.
- Perform required quality-control review.
- Present the complete applicable work and records to an authorized RTS signer.

**RTS rule**

The customer portal may display **Return to Service Complete** only after the authorized RTS action is recorded. Installation completion, invoice status, or a planned delivery date cannot trigger that status.

**Deliverable:** Completed tests, inspections, records, and authorized RTS approval.  
**Exit gate:** An authorized person approves the aircraft for return to service within the applicable authority.

### Phase 9: Deliver and Train

**Customer experience**

- Receive the aircraft, records, system explanation, account setup, connected-device setup, operating demonstration, and support contact.
- Participate in a familiarization flight when appropriate.
- Understand any deferred or open items before accepting delivery.

**RWAS execution**

- Complete the manufacturer-neutral delivery checklist.
- Apply the Garmin delivery supplement or other manufacturer supplement as applicable.
- Confirm onboard documents, configuration media, databases, software, warranties, and connected services.
- Conduct ground orientation and applicable operational demonstration.
- Conduct the familiarization flight when conditions, qualifications, and scope permit.
- Record delivery completion and customer acknowledgment.

**Familiarization limitation**

The familiarization flight is a customer familiarization and operational demonstration. It is not represented as flight instruction unless performed by an appropriately qualified instructor under a separately defined arrangement.

**Deliverable:** Aircraft delivery package, completed checklist, training record, and customer acknowledgment.  
**Exit gate:** Customer receives the aircraft, records, orientation, support path, and disclosure of any open items.

### Phase 10: Support After RTS

**48–72 hours**

- Confirm the aircraft reached its destination as expected.
- Ask about alerts, abnormal behavior, and pilot questions.
- Verify device pairing, databases, and connected services.
- Escalate anything that may affect safe operation to qualified personnel immediately.

**30 days**

- Review actual operating experience.
- Resolve configuration and training questions.
- Confirm data logging, database workflow, subscriptions, and connected services.
- Review open warranty or corrective action.
- Make preference changes only through the appropriate approved process.

**90 days**

- Conduct a project-success review.
- Confirm support issues are closed, scheduled, or assigned.
- Remind the owner of applicable ICA and maintenance obligations.
- Discuss future optimization only after the delivered project is stable.
- Request a review, testimonial, or referral only when the customer is satisfied.

**Ongoing**

- Warranty coordination
- Applicable service-alert and service-bulletin communication
- Software and database guidance
- Maintenance and future-upgrade support
- Access to the Project Advisor and retained project record

**Deliverable:** Logged follow-ups and owned resolution of open issues.  
**Exit gate:** The 90-day review is complete, with unresolved items assigned and scheduled.

## 6. Project Journey Record

Every project uses one continuous record connecting marketing intake, customer communication, the work order, delivery, and support.

### Required record groups

1. Lead source and customer contact details
2. Customer communication preferences and consent
3. Aircraft registration, year, make, model, serial number, and relevant configuration
4. Aircraft records, photos, and customer-supplied information
5. Mission brief and measurable success criteria
6. Panel Planner concept or equivalent outline and revisions
7. Technical review, eligibility path, assumptions, alternatives, and risks
8. Accepted proposal, exclusions, payment milestones, and schedule
9. Parts, receiving, traceability, staging, and backorder status
10. Aircraft induction condition and reconciled baseline
11. Work entries, milestones, photographs, and inspections
12. Discrepancies and customer approvals or declinations
13. Test, configuration, and inspection records
14. RTS documentation and authorized signoff
15. Delivery, training, account, and configuration checklist
16. 48–72-hour, 30-day, and 90-day follow-up records

### Privacy and security requirements

- Do not request or store the customer’s flyGarmin password or other account password.
- Record the customer’s account email and permission state, not credentials.
- Limit customer data, photographs, records, and approvals to authorized users.
- Keep customer PII and aircraft records out of public marketing artifacts unless separately approved.
- Preserve the approver, timestamp, scope, and evidence for customer decisions.
- Separate customer-visible status text from internal technical notes when necessary.

## 7. Customer-facing status model

The portal and communication templates use these plain-language statuses:

1. Inquiry Received
2. Discovery in Progress
3. Concept Under Review
4. Proposal Ready
5. Awaiting Your Approval
6. Scheduled and Preparing
7. Aircraft Received
8. Installation in Progress
9. Your Decision Is Needed
10. Testing and Inspection
11. Return to Service Complete
12. Ready for Delivery
13. Post-Delivery Support

### Status rules

- **Your Decision Is Needed** identifies the decision, deadline if any, and affected work.
- A project can return from testing to installation when a test or inspection fails.
- **Return to Service Complete** requires the recorded authorized RTS gate.
- **Ready for Delivery** also requires the delivery package and orientation plan to be prepared.
- **Post-Delivery Support** remains active through the 90-day review and any assigned open items.

## 8. Communication commitments

- Automatic inquiry acknowledgment
- Project Advisor introduction and next action
- Written discovery recap
- Concept-review presentation
- Proposal and schedule confirmation
- Aircraft-arrival acknowledgment
- Milestone updates and at least weekly active-work updates
- Immediate notification of material cost, schedule, scope, configuration, or airworthiness impact
- Decision reminders for blocked work
- Testing and inspection notice
- Delivery-readiness and customer-preparation notice
- 48–72-hour, 30-day, and 90-day follow-ups

Communication should be clear without exposing internal jargon. It should distinguish confirmed facts, estimates, assumptions, and decisions.

## 9. Garmin Customer Aircraft Delivery Checklist integration

**Source:** [Garmin Customer Aircraft Delivery Checklist](https://atlaske-content.garmin.com/filestorage//email/outbound/attachments/Customer_Aircraft_Delivery_Checklist_Time1761924992585.pdf), document 190-03194-00 Revision 4, dated October 2025.

The Garmin checklist is an equipment-specific supplement. It does not replace required system checkout or post-installation checkout procedures in installation manuals and approved data. Its integrated navigator, flight-display, and autopilot examples apply only when that equipment and configuration are installed.

### At aircraft drop-off

- Provide applicable Garmin training resources, including videos, webinar recordings, PC/iPad trainers, included training opportunities, and paid options.
- Capture the customer’s flyGarmin account email or help establish an account.
- Request dealer access through the applicable Garmin account process.
- Have the customer grant and acknowledge that permission.

### Before aircraft delivery

**Pilot reference material**

- Pilot’s Guide
- Cockpit Reference Guide
- Applicable AFMS with installed options identified
- Updated weight and balance in the appropriate aircraft records

**Maintenance and installation records**

- Instructions for Continued Airworthiness
- Wiring-drawing package
- Aircraft logbook entries
- Garmin STC permission letter when applicable
- FAA Form 337 or equivalent when applicable
- Weight-and-balance calculation
- Applicable authorized-release documentation

**Configuration and removable media**

- Applicable physical SD-card enablements
- System configuration-loader cards
- Saved installed-system configuration summary
- Protective storage appropriate for the aircraft records

**Software, databases, and registration**

- Correct software in installed units and connected accessories
- Applicable aviation database alerts and advisories reviewed
- Installed avionics warranty registered
- Applicable connected-device registration, activation, or trial setup completed

### With the customer on delivery day

- Update databases through the customer’s account and appropriate tools.
- Configure Database Concierge or other supported update workflow.
- Verify connected-device registration.
- Pair supported portable devices and applications.
- Confirm appropriate status for situational data, ADS-B, weather, traffic, and other installed services.
- Demonstrate two-way flight-plan transfer where supported.
- Demonstrate weather and traffic presentation where supported.
- Confirm engine data and automatic log transfer where installed.

### Familiarization and operational demonstration

Only installed and applicable functions are included. Examples include:

- Navigator/CDI-source selection
- Primary and standby display synchronization
- Fuel-flow, fuel-calculation, and range information
- GTN Xi VNAV/VCALC and applicable approach transitions
- GFC autopilot modes, disconnect behavior, ESP, yaw damper, and LVL mode
- Datalink and active traffic correlation
- Weather display and weather radar presentation
- Engine indication markings, fuel flow, and fuel quantity
- Audio panel, intercom, push-to-talk, voice commands, and speaker
- Smart Glide configuration and behavior
- Smart Rudder Bias on appropriately equipped eligible aircraft

### Completion evidence

- Aircraft registration
- Aircraft owner/customer name
- flyGarmin account email
- Garmin dealer name
- RWAS person completing the checklist
- Completion date and retained checklist record

## 10. Exception handling

| Situation | Required workflow response |
|---|---|
| Missing aircraft information | Hold technical review and request the specific missing records, photos, or identifiers. |
| Incompatible concept | Explain the conflict, preserve the customer’s goal, and present compliant alternatives. |
| Equipment backorder | Show schedule impact and offer waiting, approved substitution, or revised timing. |
| Induction discrepancy | Pause affected work, document the finding, and route it through approval. |
| Unexpected airworthiness concern | Secure the aircraft, notify qualified personnel, and follow the applicable maintenance process. |
| Customer nonresponse | Set Your Decision Is Needed, record contact attempts, and hold affected work. |
| Functional-test failure | Return to troubleshooting or installation; do not advance toward RTS. |
| Inspection finding | Correct or formally resolve the finding before release within the applicable process. |
| Missing documentation | Block RTS until the required record is complete. |
| Customer declines work | Record the decision and its effect on scope, configuration, operation, schedule, or delivery. |
| Post-delivery issue | Triage by safety, operational, warranty, configuration, and training severity; assign a named owner. |

## 11. Future customer webpage

### Selected visual direction

**Cinematic Scroll**: premium, full-width project imagery and an emotional narrative, with expandable detail that reveals the disciplined two-lane process.

### Page sections

1. **Hero — From Vision to Flight**  
   Customer promise, premium aircraft/panel footage, and primary CTA.
2. **Imagine**  
   Mission discovery, every intake path, and one Project Advisor.
3. **Design**  
   Panel Planner, aircraft data, engineering review, tradeoffs, and alternatives.
4. **Approve**  
   Defined scope, assumptions, generic payment milestones, schedule, and no-surprise change control.
5. **Prepare**  
   Parts staging, aircraft-arrival planning, records, and induction.
6. **Build**  
   Fabrication, wiring, installation, craftsmanship, milestone updates, and customer decisions.
7. **Verify and Fly**  
   Testing, inspection, complete records, authorized RTS, delivery, training, and familiarization.
8. **Supported**  
   48–72-hour, 30-day, and 90-day follow-up plus ongoing support.

### Calls to action

- **Primary:** Start Your Panel Vision — opens the customer Panel Planner.
- **Secondary:** Talk With an RWAS Project Advisor — opens the standardized avionics discovery intake.
- **Later-page CTA:** Submit Your Aircraft and Mission — begins the qualified project record.

The page may explain the customer/RWAS two-lane detail through expandable sections, but it should not expose internal-only notes or imply that a concept is an installed quote or approved configuration.

## 12. Future 75–90 second sales film

The film shares the webpage’s eight promises.

| Scene | Approximate timing | Visual direction | Message |
|---|---:|---|---|
| The Vision | 0–8 seconds | Aircraft at dawn; dark panel powers up | Every great panel begins with the way the customer flies. |
| Discovery | 8–18 seconds | Owner and advisor review the mission and Panel Planner | One advisor helps turn goals into a defined project. |
| Engineering | 18–28 seconds | Concept transitions into reviewed system planning | RWAS checks eligibility, compatibility, structure, electrical requirements, and approval path. |
| Commitment | 28–36 seconds | Customer reviews and approves a clear plan | Defined scope, schedule, milestones, and controlled changes. |
| Craftsmanship | 36–53 seconds | Fabrication, wiring, harness, and installation montage | The vision becomes aircraft-quality work, with regular customer communication. |
| Verification | 53–65 seconds | Power-up, configuration, testing, inspection, and records | The system is tested, inspected, documented, and approved for RTS by authorized personnel. |
| Delivery | 65–78 seconds | Account setup, device pairing, customer training, and familiarization flight | RWAS transfers understanding, not just equipment. |

**Emotional arc:** aspiration → trust → craftsmanship → proof → confidence → lasting relationship.

The main film can later produce shorter cutdowns for Panel Planner, craftsmanship, delivery/training, and post-RTS support.

## 13. Quality measures

RWAS should track:

- Inquiry-to-human-response time
- Discovery-to-proposal time
- Percentage of projects with complete aircraft information before technical review
- Weekly update compliance
- Number and age of customer decisions awaiting response
- Schedule variance and documented cause
- Change orders with complete customer approval evidence
- First-pass system-checkout rate
- Documentation completeness before RTS
- Delivery-checklist completion
- 48–72-hour, 30-day, and 90-day follow-up completion
- Post-delivery issues by category and time to resolution
- Customer satisfaction, testimonials, and referrals

## 14. Acceptance tests

The workflow is ready for implementation planning only when each scenario has a clear owner, record, customer message, and gate behavior.

### Scenario 1: Straightforward panel upgrade

- Lead enters through Panel Planner.
- Mission, aircraft record, concept, proposal, work order, installation, checkout, RTS, delivery, and support remain connected.

### Scenario 2: Incompatible concept

- RWAS identifies the incompatibility before proposal.
- The customer sees the reason and reasonable alternatives.
- The original goal and revision history remain preserved.

### Scenario 3: Equipment backorder

- Parts coordination detects the issue before induction when possible.
- The customer receives schedule impact and approved options.
- No substitution occurs without technical and customer approval.

### Scenario 4: Significant induction discrepancy

- A quoted assumption differs from the actual aircraft.
- Affected work pauses.
- The customer receives evidence, options, price, and schedule impact.
- The project resumes only after a recorded decision.

### Scenario 5: Failed system checkout

- The project moves from Testing and Inspection back to active troubleshooting.
- The portal does not display RTS complete.
- The customer receives a revised status and impact when material.

### Scenario 6: Post-RTS concern

- The customer reports a concern after delivery.
- RWAS triages safety, operational, warranty, configuration, and training implications.
- A named owner and response path are recorded.
- Follow-up remains open until resolved or scheduled.

### Additional acceptance criteria

- No stage can advance without its required exit gate.
- The customer always knows the next step and whether a decision is required.
- The Project Advisor remains visible throughout the lifecycle.
- Customer-facing and internal status remain reconcilable.
- No additional billable scope proceeds without approval evidence.
- RTS status cannot be set without authorized RTS evidence.
- Garmin delivery activities are configuration-specific and do not replace approved checkout procedures.
- Post-RTS support touches are scheduled at delivery and retained in the project record.

## 15. Implementation boundaries

This document defines the approved workflow and experience. It does not yet authorize or specify:

- Exact payment percentages or financial terms
- Staffing assignments by individual name
- Automatic promises of project duration
- A warranty duration beyond applicable manufacturer and RWAS policy
- Customer access to internal-only maintenance notes
- Automatic RTS or release decisions
- Public deployment of the webpage
- Production of the sales film

Those items require a separate implementation plan and, where applicable, business or regulatory approval.
