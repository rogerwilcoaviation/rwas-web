# RWAS Comprehensive SEO Audit and Remediation

**Audit date:** October 3, 2026 (America/New_York)

**Site:** https://www.rogerwilcoaviation.com

**Repository:** `rwas-web`

**Scope:** Technical SEO, crawl/indexation, on-page metadata, headings, structured data, internal links, images, performance, accessibility, Search Console landing pages, local SEO, and AI-discovery files.

## Executive summary

RWAS has a strong technical foundation. The production sitemap contained 1,899 indexable URLs and every URL returned a successful response. The crawl found no broken internal links, orphan sitemap pages, missing canonicals, duplicate titles, invalid JSON-LD, missing viewport tags, or materially thin pages.

The audit did identify several template-level defects and one material search-traffic leak:

- Search Console still sent meaningful demand to retired product pages that returned 404, led by the former Forerunner 955 Solar page with 1,170 impressions and 10 clicks in the audited 28-day period.
- Product and service templates skipped heading levels on 1,582 rendered pages.
- Six newly imported Garmin products shared the same availability-only meta description.
- Product, blog, and service images frequently lacked explicit intrinsic dimensions, including the image causing a measured 0.067 CLS on the highest-impression blog article.
- The homepage shipped oversized LCP, aircraft, video-poster, and logo assets.
- `robots.txt` contained a nonstandard `Content-signal` directive that Lighthouse treated as an error on every page.
- The Yankton location and full product-index pages inherited generic social metadata.
- `llms.txt` labeled a Yankton location link but linked to the general services directory.
- The catalog audit mislabeled 520 deliberate quote-only Product-schema omissions as errors.

All safe repository-level fixes were implemented and validated. External directory corrections still require account-owner updates.

Of 1,437 Search Console landing pages, 145 currently returned 404. Together they represented 18 clicks and 1,860 impressions. Most were very low-volume discontinued watch-band variants and correctly remain 404.

Fourteen URLs with meaningful demand or a clear one-to-one successor now have permanent redirects. They account for 12 clicks and 1,496 impressions in the audit window. The largest recovery is:

Other high-demand retired watch and accessory URLs redirect either to a direct successor or the tightly related Watches & Accessories collection. Unrelated retired URLs were not mass-redirected, avoiding soft-404 behavior.

## On-page and metadata findings

- Titles were unique across all 1,899 pages.
- The crawler flagged 468 titles and 782 descriptions as shorter than generic heuristic thresholds. These are not blanket defects: the snippets are unique, descriptive, and many already rank well. No bulk padding was applied.
- Six Garmin portable products shared an availability-only description. The product metadata helper now prefixes the product and vendor identity while preserving the availability notice.
- Yankton and Product Index now emit page-specific Open Graph and Twitter title, description, URL, and image metadata.
- Social metadata was present across the crawl.
- Every page had one H1. The product specification card was promoted from H3 to H2, and 13 rail-card headings were changed from H4 to H3. This removes the recurring H1/H2-to-H3/H4 skip without changing visual styling.

### Remediation

- Added intrinsic dimensions to all shared Specimen images, product lightboxes, gallery thumbnails, article heroes, article lightboxes, and inline article images.
- Defaulted shared editorial images to lazy loading and asynchronous decoding while preserving eager/high-priority loading for true LCP images.
- Corrected image-zoom accessible names so they include the visible “Click image to enlarge” label.
- Increased inline phone/email touch targets to at least 24px.
- Replaced oversized homepage AXIS, fabrication-poster, aircraft-thumbnail, masthead-logo, service, and product-placeholder deliveries with right-sized WebP assets or responsive API variants.
- Reduced the homepage AXIS image from 178 KB to 23 KB, the fabrication poster from 74 KB to 5 KB, and the masthead logo from 49 KB to 8 KB. Optimized service and fallback images save hundreds of additional kilobytes when those assets are used.
- Removed the unsupported `Content-signal` line while retaining RWAS's explicit crawler rules and Cloudflare enforcement comments.

The PageSpeed Insights API daily quota was exhausted, so the performance sample used Lighthouse 12.8.2 in mobile mode with local Chrome. Final production measurements should be treated as lab data and monitored alongside Search Console and field CWV.
